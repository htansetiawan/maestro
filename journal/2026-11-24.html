Chopin's nocturne, sung on the tune as I play it

Chopin's Nocturne in C-sharp minor over Bruce Liu's live recording, the theme (bars 5 to 20): the tune I played on a MIDI keyboard over his piano, 55 notes in one take, now carries the draft words and is sung by six AI voices. Chopin's Nocturne in C-sharp minor was written for piano alone; its tune is the top line of the right hand. To give that tune to a voice over Bruce Liu's live recording, there are two ways to get the line: read it from a transcription of his playing, or play it myself on a MIDI keyboard while his recording runs. The first gave an exact copy of his right hand that a singer could not carry. The second is now on the canvas, the theme (bars 5 to 20) as a research excerpt, sung with draft words by six AI voices.

Reading the tune versus playing it

Way What happened
Read the top line of a transcription of Liu's right hand Exact, but it is a pianist's line: trills, turns and fast runs had to be left to the piano, and the voice held the note before each one
Play it on a MIDI keyboard over the recording Kept: one take of 55 notes over the 16 bars, an octave below his hand, with the ornaments simplified to what a voice can carry

The take keeps a few turns (on is, burn-ing, low, a-cross) and the falling run of bar 15 as one long slur on road, from A down to the low A. Liu plays freely in time, so the bars come from a bar map placed by hand on the notes that begin each bar, not from a fixed tempo; the recorder now counts bars of uneven length the same way (the middle section is in 3/4) and spells notes in the key, C# rather than Db.

Fitting the words to the take

The draft words (38 syllables, written by the agent for this line; they are not Chopin's) were placed on the 55 notes:

  • Onsets moved to the piano. 31 of my note starts were moved onto Liu's nearest note of the same name, never more than a quarter of a second and never past a neighbouring note, so the voice lands with his hand.
  • Notes carried within a syllable. 18 notes are slurs: the syllable before them is carried through a turn or a run.
  • One note split. Fall-ing has two syllables and I played one held G#; it is split on beat 2 and shown in gold. Every other note is blue, mine as played.

How the words sit on the notes turned out to matter more to the singer than which notes they are:

  • A turn on the first syllable made the singer repeat the word. With a three-note turn on burn, every voice sang "burning, burning"; with the same turn moved onto -ing, every voice sang the word once. A single slide on a first syllable (car-ry, C# down to C) did no harm.
  • A gap inside a word splits it. A short rest between o and ver came out as "Oh, for". Notes inside a word now run into each other with no gap.
  • A syllable left alone before a long rest is lost. The sat two seconds before river and was dropped. Wall now rises an octave, C# to C#, and the moves onto the first note of the next phrase.

The voices

Six voices, each an AI voice model made from a short reference to a published recording, not the singer: Josh Groban, Luciano Pavarotti, Michael Bolton, Bryan Adams, Ariana Grande, and mine (from my song The Yard). The key finder put the men four semitones down: Bolton and Pavarotti land there by its rule, and the piano is moved down with them. For Ariana Grande the rule said two down; my ear has preferred women's voices above that rule, so she sings in the recording's key. Pavarotti's reference is in Italian, which the singing model cannot sing from a score, so his voice is the Groban take re-voiced by voice conversion.

Every take was checked with Whisper for missed words before it went on the page:

Voice Key Takes made Words Whisper did not hear (of 38)
Josh Groban piano down 4 2, joined at a rest road
Luciano Pavarotti, by voice conversion piano down 4 7 conversions over, low, tonight, I'll, carry
Michael Bolton piano down 4 2 road
Bryan Adams piano down 4 2 town
Ariana Grande the recording's key 4 I'll
Mine an octave down 2 road, I'll

Road, carried over eight notes down to the low A, is the word lost most often, by three of the six voices: a long run on one vowel leaves little of the final consonant. Pavarotti's voice keeps the notes but blurs the English; no conversion tried, from three of his clips and two conversion models, did better than five missed words. One entry is early in every voice: I'll in bar 13 comes in about 0.2 seconds before the piano. Night, the first word, comes in about 0.2 seconds late for Ariana Grande and Pavarotti.
