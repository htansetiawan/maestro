  1. October 24, 2026

    Four score readers on one ruler: the notes and the structure live in different engines

    Four released score readers on the same pages and graders: homr reads the most notes on every set (100% on rendered truth, 91.7% of right-hand notes on 200 real IMSLP scans) but has the worst tree-edit score (0.475); Zeus, a 2024 model trained on renders of the same Lieder corpus, scores 0.144 with fewer notes right; the 2026 full-page models Transcoda and LEGATO are third and fourth on every set, with runaway decoding on dense commercial pages.

  2. October 20, 2026

    Music Transformer, reproduced and running in the browser

    The released Music Transformer configuration, re-implemented in PyTorch and trained for one million steps on the official MAESTRO v1 split, reaches validation NLL 1.835 on the selection metric (the paper reports 1.835), 1.827 on every validation event and 1.801 on test; the port matches the original Tensor2Tensor code to float32 rounding, and the trained model now runs in the browser at 29 ms per event with a pianist-or-model blind test.

  3. October 18, 2026

    What the reader keeps and what the singer keeps

    On clean engravings open OMR reads both hands and the chord tones completely (homr 100% of K. 545, also from a phone photograph), so the 79–86% on commercial sheets is a layout and typography gap; YuE2 sings She in register but keeps 37% of the line against a 71% ceiling; MusicGen-melody, with no notation input, follows the same lines better (96% of She in order, an 18-note run), and EnCodec loses none of the harmony.

  4. October 17, 2026

    Where the score gets lost

    YuE2's score input is a lead sheet: a third of the printed notes can be sent at all, OMR reads the melody but no chord symbols, the melody that is sent comes back loosely (80% recall alone, worse with chord symbols), and Stable Audio Open keeps the recording's harmony but not its register.

  7. October 4, 2026

    Music to score, score to music: the anatomy of a song generator

    Three Suno uploads of one piano recording came back as audio and nothing readable. A technical report dissects why — codec-token language models through four lenses — and specifies the inverse: a notation-native latent that transcribes in and renders out, with latent actions as the edit primitive.
