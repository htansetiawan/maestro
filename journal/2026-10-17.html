Weekly placeholder — October 17, 2026. This week I am studying how music moves between sound, notation and model inputs. The question is where the musical information gets lost before or during generation.

Reading this week

  • Meinard Müller, Fundamentals of Music Processing: Audio, Analysis, Algorithms, Applications, Springer, 2015, first edition. Chapter 1: Music Representations — sheet music, symbolic representations and audio. Book · Companion notebooks.
  • Jade Copet et al., Simple and Controllable Music Generation (MusicGen), NeurIPS 2023. Reading for its discrete audio tokens and text/melody conditioning. Paper.
  • Alexandre Défossez et al., High Fidelity Neural Audio Compression (EnCodec), 2022. Reading for the audio representation underlying MusicGen. Paper.

I added a researcher directory: the AudioCraft/MusicGen and Jukebox/MuseNet contributors; Anna Huang, Eran Egozy, Joseph Paradiso and Tod Machover at MIT; Chris Donahue, Hao-Wen Dong, Xu Tan, Tie-Yan Liu and Meinard Müller. It connects their work to representation, generation and musical interaction.

Investigation planned

Two input paths need separate tests:

PDF/image → score reading → MusicXML → checked musical events → ABC
Sound/humming → transcription → checked musical events → ABC/MusicXML

First check whether the notes, rhythms and rests survive conversion. For piano scores, track the melody, left hand, chord tones and bass separately. Then test simple melody imitation and an isolated vocal before adding accompaniment.

The earlier Color Purple trial used a manual melody/chord reduction, not the full piano arrangement. The documented YuE2 input workflow uses two monophonic lines plus chord symbols; that does not preserve every written piano note. Whether it follows even the retained melody in the audio remains the test.

These are prepared experiments and plans, not new quality results. The separate October 19 draft remains unfinished.
