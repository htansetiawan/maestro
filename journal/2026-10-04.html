Music to score, score to music: the anatomy of a song generator

Three Suno uploads of one piano recording came back as audio and nothing readable. A technical report dissects why — codec-token language models through four lenses — and specifies the inverse: a notation-native latent that transcribes in and renders out, with latent actions as the edit primitive.

October 4, 2026 · Henry Tan · Entry source

Last month I uploaded a solo piano recording of mine to Suno three times. I asked for a vocal line over it, then for an orchestral arrangement, then for a clean instrumental. Each time a plausible recording came back, and each time nothing I could read came with it: no lead sheet for the melody the singer sang, no short score for the strings, no indication of which of my voicings had been kept and which quietly re-harmonised. This week's work is a technical report that takes that experience apart. What does a Suno-class system represent internally, why does that representation keep the musician out, and what would it take to build the inverse: a system whose shared representation is the score, so that audio can be transcribed into it and notation rendered out of it under human control.

What changed

The report exists. Twelve sections: an introduction built on the three Suno uploads, background, the anatomy through four lenses, the problem statement for bidirectional music ↔ score, one methodology section for each direction, a proposal for a notation-native latent with its objective written out, latent actions as the edit primitive, the case study revisited, an evaluation plan, limitations, and a conclusion. It is a proposal, and it says so; nothing in it has been trained.

Four lenses from outside music. I read the Suno family through the ideas that produced it. From text transformers: everything is next-token prediction over a discrete vocabulary, and Bark, YuE, MusicGen and Jukebox are all language models over codec tokens. From VQ-VAE: the vocabulary is learned, and learned hierarchically, so Jukebox's three resolutions and Bark's semantic → coarse → fine stages are the same shape. From ViT: universal features come from patches and transfer, which is why MERT and MuQ sit under so many of these systems as the condition encoder. From Genie: actions can be learned without labels from pairs of frames, which turns out to be the most useful idea in the report for Maestro.

A comparative anatomy from primary sources. One table collects what the papers, model cards and READMEs actually confirm about Suno (inferred from Bark), Bark, YuE, ACE-Step 1.5, Magenta RealTime 2, MusicGen, Jukebox and JASCO: tokeniser, prior, conditions, region editing, symbolic input and licence. Two patterns fall out of it. Symbolic input is the exception, and the only two systems that accept anything a musician would call notation (Magenta RT 2's live MIDI, JASCO's chord labels) are research systems. Region editing arrived from the product side, not the architecture side; ACE-Step's repainting is the first design in the family where editing a region is what the model natively does.

Corrections to things I had been repeating. The verification pass fixed several numbers that circulate in secondary sources: ACE-Step 1.0 is Apache-2.0, not MIT (1.5 is MIT); Magenta RT v1 is 220M and 770M in the paper rather than the blog's 800M, and MRT2 is 230M and 2.4B, decoder-only; Genie's dataset is 30k curated hours, not the 200k-hour pool, and its latent action codebook has eight entries; PM2S is by Liu, Kong, Morfi and Benetos, not Foscarin; DiffRhythm's parameter count is not published anywhere primary. These are now in the report and in the bib.

The site carries it. The report is a doc row on the landing page, a Report link in the shared navigation template, and a built artifact in the journal system. MathJax rendering was added to the document template so the objective in §7.4 reads as equations rather than source.

Current thinking

The blind spot of the Suno family is structural, not a missing feature. The representation between condition and output is a hierarchy of codec tokens, rich enough to carry key, harmony and form, and it is discarded on the way to the WAV. That is why the operations I actually needed (see the melody, check the chords, keep my voicings) are the ones the architecture cannot expose, and why the editing features added since 2024 are retrofits.

The inverse is buildable from parts that exist. Audio to note events is close to solved; note events to notation is where current systems stop and where the human has the most leverage, since a single confirmation of meter or key lifts alignment accuracy from 33% to 82% in the Bukey study. Expressive rendering and synthesis are mature. Symbolic-conditioned generation exists in JASCO, Coco-Mulla and Music ControlNet and is waiting for a score to condition on. Variational latents over symbolic music exist in MusicVAE and PianoTree-VAE; contrastive alignment of scores, performances and audio exists in CLaMP 3. What does not exist is the conjunction.

So the proposal is a notation-native latent: a hierarchical VAE over bar-patched notation (interleaved ABC or **kern, required to round-trip losslessly to MusicXML or MEI), with the bar level as the part a human edits and the part that aligns contrastively to frozen MERT or MuQ embeddings. The objective has four terms, reconstruction plus alignment plus an explicit transcription bridge plus a notation-consistency penalty, and the rendering side is handled by feeding the trained latent to a symbolic-conditioned generator in place of its existing condition. Above it, following Genie, a latent action model trained on pairs of musical versions would learn edit operations without labels, and a composer's annotations become the human-readable names for those actions. That last move is the one that connects this report to the reward module from September: the annotation is the instruction, the action is what the model learned to do, and the build log is the diff between them.

I am also clear about what is not new. Not the VAE, not the contrastive alignment, not the bar patching. The claim is only about the conjunction, and the obvious baseline (bolt a decoder onto CLaMP 3) has to be run and reported first.

Findings and evidence

  • Suno's internals are inferred from Bark and one 2024 interview; the release notes for v5 and v6 contain no architecture statements. If production Suno has moved toward diffusion, §3's inference about it is wrong, though the anatomy of the open systems stands on its own.
  • CLaMP 3's emergent sheet → audio retrieval MRR is 0.058. I read this as a hardness number: a retrieval-grade embedding that discriminates pieces this coarsely is unlikely to carry what a decoder needs to reconstruct them, which is the argument for a VAE posterior rather than a contrastive tower.
  • Jung et al. 2025 looked like a unified audio-to-notation system, but its audio → symbolic path emits MIDI-like tokens; the notation (Linearized MusicXML) comes from the OMR path. The two-stage problem remains two stages.
  • The three Suno operations map onto the framework differently. Add vocals is a rendering step on an augmented score. Orchestral version is a latent action applied to the transcribed score. Instrumental is the trivial case, the score minus a staff, a one-line edit with no model call. The operations that are expensive in codec-token space are cheap on a score, and the one Suno needed a feature for is the one that costs nothing in notation.
  • Of about 49 primary-source lookups, the items that stayed unverified are mostly parameter counts (DiffRhythm, YuE's exact stage-1 size, MuQ) and venues; nothing load-bearing in the argument rests on an unverified claim.

Open questions

  • Can a text notation encoding round-trip losslessly to MusicXML or MEI? Both **kern and interleaved ABC lose layout and some markings today, and the whole proposal rests on what the latent decodes being what a notation editor opens.
  • Does the Genie analogy survive contact with musical version pairs? Video frames are regular and dense; pairs of arrangements are irregular, sparse, and often differ in length. The latent action model may need a different inductive bias.
  • Will the first version transcribe worse than a tuned two-stage pipeline and render worse than a sampled piano, because it is doing both at once? My expectation is yes. The question is whether the round-trip and editability advantages are worth that cost early on.
  • How does this sit with the ACE-Step experiment already under way? ACE is an audio-latent system of exactly the kind the report dissects. The adapter work teaches me what the audio side can learn from Foster; the report argues that the part he would want to read back is a different object. The two threads should meet at the conditioning interface in §6.2.

Next experiment

Run the baseline the report says must come first: a decoder on top of CLaMP 3's symbolic embedding, measured on reconstruction and on retrieval, to see how much of the score a retrieval-grade latent actually carries. In parallel, settle the notation encoding by testing round-trip fidelity of **kern and interleaved ABC against a sample of PDMX scores through MusicXML, and record the loss. Then take my own piano recording through the two-stage transcription path (note events, then PM2S or the Beyer and Dai transformer), confirm meter and key by hand, and keep the resulting score as the first fixture for the case study: upload the piano, see the score, add the voice as notes, read the build log.
