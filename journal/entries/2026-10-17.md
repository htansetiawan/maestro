# Where the score gets lost

Last week's question was why conditioning YuE2 on the printed *Theme from The Color Purple* did not give back the arrangement. This week locates the loss. Most of it is structural: the model's score input is a lead sheet, not a score. The rest is measured for the first time.

## What YuE2 can receive

YuE2 accepts a bounded ABC dialect: two monophonic voices, `Vocal` and `Ins`, plus quoted chord symbols from fifteen qualities, on a power-of-two rhythmic grid with no tuplets, grace notes, note stacks, repeats or expression marks. A piano arrangement cannot be sent as notes. The left hand and the chord voicings reach the model only as labels, and the accompaniment is always the model's own.

Counting the printed notes from the best OMR reading: 838 sounding notes on the five pages, of which 276, the top of the right hand, can be expressed in the model's input. That is a third. The other two thirds, 189 right-hand chord tones and 373 left-hand notes, were never in play in experiment 007.

## Can software read the sheet

| Engine | Measures found (of 88) | Melody recall | Written octave | Chord symbols (of 98) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| homr 0.7 | 83 | 86% | 85% | 0 |
| oemer 0.1.8 | 84 | 73% | 69% | 0 |
| Audiveris 5.11 | 77 | 50% | 45% | 0 |

homr read four of the five pages completely and lost five measures on page 4. No engine read a chord symbol, so the harmony still has to come from a person. The manual lead line from experiment 007 holds up as the reference: against the human recording, the same alignment method finds 79% of its notes over the whole piece and 96% over the first 17 bars.

## Does the model follow the melody it is given

Seventeen bars, one seed each, first 60 seconds graded by transcribing the output and aligning its top line to the written melody. The pianist's own recording scores 96% recall with a longest exact run of 13 notes.

| What the score carried | Melody recall | Written octave | Longest exact run | Tempo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melody only | 80% | 63% | 10 | 1.33× |
| Melody + chord symbols (007 recipe), two seeds | 67%, 67% | 14%, 39% | 2, 5 | 0.96×, 1.28× |
| Melody + chords + bass line in the Vocal voice | 75% | 45% | 7 | 1.10× |
| No score | 53% | 35% | 4 | 1.18× |

Three things follow. The melody alone is imitated loosely but recognisably: four notes in five, ten in a row in the right octave, a third slower than marked. Adding chord symbols, which is what 007 did, made the following worse in both seeds; the two seeds also disagree enough that this is a direction rather than a ranking. The bass line in the second voice was sung: Whisper hears a long run of "ah" syllables despite the no-vocals prompt, so the second voice cannot carry an instrument. Four of the five clips also ran to the token cap at 140 s instead of stopping where the 44-second score stops.

Grading last week's 007 output with the same method gives 63% recall, 18% in the written octave, runs of at most 7 notes, and a 1.53× stretch that matches its 5:39 against a 3:49 score. The impression from listening, that the piano had changed too much, now has a number.

## A model with no score input, for contrast

Stable Audio Open 1.0 takes text, or text plus a starting recording, and nothing symbolic. From the piano prompt alone it produces piano that shares 35% of the recording's top-line pitch classes in order and 2–5% in the right octave, which is the floor for unrelated music in the same style. Starting from the recording itself, the outcome depends on how much noise is added before sampling:

| Start | Noise level σ | Chroma similarity to the recording | Top-line notes kept | In the right octave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recording, piano prompt | 0.5 | 0.99 | 71% | 51% |
| Recording, piano prompt | 2 | 0.92 | 76% | 55% |
| Recording, piano prompt | 8 | 0.54 | 43% | 21% |
| Recording, orchestra prompt | 2 | 0.93 | 74% | 53% |
| Recording, orchestra prompt | 8 | 0.56 | 48% | 16% |

Even the lightest pass, which keeps the harmony almost exactly, loses half the top-line notes' octaves on the way through the autoencoder and sampler. At σ = 2 the harmony survives under either prompt; at σ = 8 the piece is mostly regenerated. Whether the orchestra prompt added an orchestra rather than a different piano is a listening question. Each clip took 20 seconds to render.

## What this changes

The composer's path from a printed arrangement is image, then MusicXML through OMR, then a reduction to whatever the model accepts, and the reduction is where the arrangement is lost. For Music Kitchen the implication is that a score-conditioned generator needs an interface that accepts at least two hands and voicings as notes, or the product must treat the model as an accompanist to a melody and the arrangement as the person's work. Measuring adherence rather than describing it is now routine: the transcription-and-alignment script runs in seconds on the GPU and applies to any piano clip.
