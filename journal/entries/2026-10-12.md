# YuE2 experiments in musical control

After last week's ACE-Step trials fell short of my listening expectations, I tested YuE2 for Music Kitchen: can it produce useful music while keeping the composer's melody and performance under control? These were inference experiments with existing checkpoints, not model training.

**My listening assessment:** YuE2's original song from a style prompt and lyrics was acceptable. The attempts to follow a supplied score or add a voice to existing piano were less convincing. Musical quality and musical control remain separate problems.

## What I tested

| Experiment | Input and method | Output |
|---|---|---|
| [Short melody test](../../experiments/006_yue2_score_control/index.html) | Supplied 32-note motif versus a model-composed melody; same lyrics and style, two seeds. | Four initial candidates and one rerender with a larger token allowance. Audible note adherence remains unmeasured. |
| [Printed score to piano](../../experiments/007_color_purple_sheet_yue2/index.html) | Manual melody/chord reduction of the five-page *Theme from The Color Purple* score, converted to ABC notation. | 5:39 of generated piano. This represents the lead line and chords, not every part of the printed arrangement. |
| [Piano and voice](../../experiments/008_color_purple_two_vocal_versions/index.html) | A: generate piano and singing together. B: extract A's vocal, adjust its timing, and mix it over the piano generated in the previous experiment. | A: 6:12. B: 5:39. B preserves the decoded piano; vocal separation and stretching introduce additional limitations. |
| [Original Yard song](../../experiments/009_yard_original_song/index.html) | Style prompt and original lyrics; no supplied melody, score or recording. | 3:09. My preferred result: acceptable musical quality. |
| [Score with orchestral arrangement](../../experiments/010_yard_sheet_orchestral/index.html) | Supplied melody/chords and adapted lyrics, with an orchestral style prompt. | 6:11. The model generates piano, voice and accompaniment together. |

## What I learned

The original song is the strongest demonstration so far, but YuE2 chooses its melody and harmony. That is useful for generating a song; it does not establish control over a composition I already have in mind.

In my listening, the attempted arrangement changed the piano too much, and the voice felt more like an incongruent background singer than an integrated lead. It was not simply out of tune. The musical relationship between the parts was the problem.

“LEGO” describes the intended task here, not a native YuE2 operation tested in these runs. The orchestral version regenerated the performance from notation. Only the separate extract–align–mix workflow preserved an existing piano waveform. These methods should not be conflated.

Saved scores matched their supplied inputs, but that does not prove the audio followed those notes. The trials also changed lyrics, style and other settings, so they do not establish a controlled ranking of YuE2 against ACE-Step or Suno.

## Planned investigation

Updated October 12: I plan to investigate why conditioning YuE2 on *Theme from The Color Purple* did not give the result I wanted, starting with melody imitation before adding voices or orchestration.

1. **Check score reading.** Does conversion from the PDF or score image to MusicXML preserve the notes, rhythms, rests and bar structure? The earlier trial used a manual reduction to ABC; it did not validate PDF-to-MusicXML conversion.
2. **Check format conversion.** Compare a reviewed short melody across MusicXML, MIDI and the ABC input used by YuE2. Identify any information lost before generation.
3. **Test imitation.** Can YuE2 reproduce that simple melody from a verified score, or through an audio-conditioning workflow using my solo-piano recording? Assess pitches, rhythm, omissions and repeats separately from sound quality. Confirm the supported input paths rather than assume these formats are interchangeable model inputs.

The aim is to locate the failure: score reading, conversion, or generation. This is the next research plan; these tests have not yet been run.
