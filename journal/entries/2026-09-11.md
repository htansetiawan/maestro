# From survey to first experiment: what AI cannot learn from David Foster

*Draft. Written with Claude from the week's working sessions; review before publishing.*

The week started with a reading list of about a dozen seeds and ended with a decision about what to build first. In between: a survey of the field, a public site, a positioning one-pager, and a reward function that encodes the one thing I care most about — that a machine compiling my music must not overwrite the notes I pinned.

## What changed

The survey is done, for a first pass. 465 annotated entries across eight research clusters, 1957–2026, each carrying a verification level (373 verified against a primary source, 85 partial, 5 unverified), synthesized into a 43,900-word review, a 49-node taxonomy, and a 394-record annotated bibliography. Two independent QA passes found 16 external corrections and 70 internal findings; 56 fixes were applied. The corrections that changed my thinking: the Expressive Communication result is *symmetric* — interface and model gains are complementary, not ranked — and the humming-transcription gap I'd been quoting was an artefact of defective ground-truth labels.

The taxonomy's central finding is a count, not an opinion. Sorting the works by where they sit in a composer's loop — compose, annotate, compile, edit — shows 125 primary entries on *compile* and 27 on *annotate*, 25 on *edit*. The nodes that matter most are nearly empty: annotations as machine instructions (four entries, none about music), the machine explaining what it did (two), symbolic editing and proofreading (two). Every piece needed to fill the annotation gap already exists in adjacent fields and has never been assembled for music.

The experiment direction is set: **piano only**, melody → harmony → voicing → stylistic transformation, on a corpus I record myself at the Nord — David Foster material played by hand, plus non-Foster material as a control so Foster-specific can be separated from performer-specific.

## Current thinking

Can AI match a sixteen-time Grammy winner? The question decomposes. Foster is pianist, composer, arranger, producer and talent-shaper, and the awards are mostly for the last three. Surface style at the piano is learnable and has been since Cope's EMI in the 1990s. What models cannot do sorts into *not yet* — form at song scale, the runway before the key change — and *not in this architecture* — intent. No model represents *why*. The Foster modulation is learned as a token statistic and, trained on him, becomes a tic. Also outside scope: taste as selection, arrangement, artist-matching. Does AI read it? Notation, yes. Meaning, no.

The reframe that came out of this: **imitation is the diagnostic, not the goal.** Fine-tune to imitate; find precisely where experts catch it; those failure modes will cluster on form and intent — which are exactly what a composer's annotations supply. What imitation cannot learn from Foster tells me what the annotation layer must carry. The book is not *AI Learns David Foster*; it is *What AI Couldn't Learn From David Foster*.

The build plan became a research question in the same conversation. "Fine-tune on a composer, sound like them" has been done (MidiMe 2019, MIDI-RWKV 2025). The question inside my plan: *can a composer's style be captured and steered as separable layers rather than as one monolithic style vector, and does layered control give composers more of what they want than end-to-end transfer?* Foster and bebop become instances. Bebop transformation is the sharpest instance — a transformation of given material, in a taxonomy node with two prior works, with nameable, checkable features.

On architecture: I first heard "don't reimplement Music Transformer, use the Anticipatory Music Transformer." I pushed back — the goal is to learn, I have built TensorFlow, and owning the codebase is what makes every later modification mine. That stands. The paper never states a parameter count; the published config works out to roughly 20–40M, and the one hard idea is the skewing trick that takes relative attention from O(L²·D) to O(L·D) memory. Plan: naive implementation as executable spec, skewed version must pass an equivalence test. Then graft anticipation on. Then swap relative attention for RoPE, which Wang's 2024 Aalto thesis found more musical on the same task.

## Findings and evidence

- Infilling under hard constraints is mature and permissively licensed — Anticipatory Music Transformer (Apache 2.0, 128M–360M), MIDI-GPT, Composer's Assistant. The one deployment with telemetry, Hookpad Aria, reports roughly 23% of 318,000 span-level suggestions accepted inside a notation editor. [Survey §6.2](../../survey/literature-review.html)
- Steering interfaces move control and ownership even with the model fixed; a single slider is not control — MMM-C's users wanted more control at 9.5/10 while rating usability acceptable. [Survey §10.1](../../survey/literature-review.html)
- Music Transformer emits events, not audio. MIDI → renderer → WAV is a separate stage, and the separation is a feature: the symbolic layer stays editable. The Nord's own piano engine is the best renderer I have.
- Wang (Aalto, 2024) reaches anticipation behaviour by padding away part of the chord sequence during training, so the model generates melody while harmony lags — a cheap alternative to RL. Entered at S5.3 as a master's thesis, indicative.

## Open questions

- **The reward tiering.** Strict chord-tone membership scores an F over Ebmaj7 as a miss; it is a colour tone. Lush extended harmony is exactly the idiom at stake. Chord tone / colour tone / avoid note, weighted by metrical position — first real problem.
- **Autoregressive or diffusion?** Raised at the end of the week. Diffusion inpainting is the compile primitive with no anticipation machinery, and Stochastic Control Guidance would turn `rewards.py` into inference-time constraints without RL — but piano-roll diffusion discards the 10 ms timing and velocity that carry the performance DNA in my Nord captures. The representation decides the architecture, and I have not decided the representation.
- Style-similarity evaluation is unsolved; "sounds like Foster" needs nameable-device detection, expert listening, then classifiers — planned before training, not after.
- How much of Foster survives a piano reduction.
- Whether the layered-control claim is true. That is the thesis.

## Next experiment

The harness: MAESTRO/JSB pipeline with a lossless MIDI round-trip test, a naive relative-attention reference, and the equivalence test a skewed implementation must pass. The Nord capture protocol. Decide the representation — event tokens or piano roll — with real data in hand, because that decision picks the architecture.
