# Audio to score and a test of symbolic control

The research priority is an editable representation between musical sound and generation. Recent ACE-Step experiments produced realistic vocal timbre but did not satisfy the author's listening criteria for alignment, continuation, or orchestral accompaniment. The next evaluation asks whether supplying explicit notes gives more useful control.

## What the paper contributes

Cummins and colleagues decode score symbols from frozen MuQ features using a Transformer. SheetSage-A2S supplies 9,468 clips from 6,066 songs, about 61 hours, with singing-melody and chord annotations. Its lead-sheet target uses `**kern` and a chord spine. Reported SER is 20.92% on this dataset and 4.98% on synthetic Quartets; these are different tasks. The popular-music setting does not recover every instrumental staff. Annotation variability, note durations, and complex chords remain difficulties. [Paper, sections 2–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06165).

The [official implementation](https://github.com/Multimodal-Music-Research-Lab/SheetSage2Kern_model) provides separate lead-sheet and quartet checkpoints. It reports melody SER of 38.62% and chord SER of 22.28%; the overall score includes easier structural symbols. These figures should guide error inspection, rather than be read as a percentage of fully correct songs.

## Implications for Maestro

A direct notation decoder belongs beside the modular audio → note events → score baseline. Our experiment should compare both against the same manually reviewed passage. Evaluate pitch, rhythm, spelling, rests, and harmony separately; keep raw predictions alongside any human corrections.

For the compiler design, use explicit adapters between `**kern`, an internal event representation, and the ABC subset accepted by YuE2. Test preservation of pitches, rational durations, voices, and chord meaning. Record unsupported constructs instead of silently dropping them. Neither shared musical content nor similar project names establish format compatibility: SheetSage-A2S and YuE2's SheetSage2 transcription workflow are distinct projects.

The proposed research loop is:

```text
audio → estimated notation → reviewed musical events → edited score → generated audio
                    ↘ uncertainty and correction record ↗
```

Round-trip transcription alone is an insufficient judge: the transcriber and generator can share mistakes. Use an independent reference score and human listening. A singer can sound natural while singing the wrong notes; a correct score can render with unconvincing phrasing. Those are separate outcomes.

## Current evaluation

Four initial YuE2 candidates have rendered, and the listening evaluation is ready locally. Four candidates are predeclared: two seeds, each with a supplied melody and a model-composed melody, keeping lyrics, style, and melody-only mode fixed. The supplied score repeats the author's 16-note scale-degree motif twice. C major, 90 BPM, and quarter notes are illustrative assumptions, not a transcription of the Color Purple recording. The target contains 32 notes and lasts 21.33 seconds before any generated introduction or ending.

This tests score-to-audio control and musical coherence. It does not test waveform-preserving LEGO, transcription of the source piano, or superiority to Suno. The original four outputs are retained. One hit the initial token cap; rerendering its exact saved score with a larger cap produced a complete 79.96-second output. The supplied-score runs lasted 49.96 and 24.32 seconds. ASR suggests the longer one repeats the lyrics; listening must confirm that and judge melody adherence. Both model-composed scores depart from the requested 90 BPM, choosing 72 and 65 BPM. Listening judgments remain pending. [Open the comparison](../../experiments/006_yue2_score_control/index.html).
