Where AI Music Generation Stands, and Where the Openings Are

A research-positioning one-pager, drawn from the Maestro survey of 463 works (1957–2026)

1. The state of the field in one paragraph

Generation is no longer the bottleneck. Text-to-audio song generation works at consumer quality, commercially and in the open (Suno Inc. 2026; Universal Music Group and Udio 2025; Google DeepMind 2025; Gong et al. 2026; Ning et al. 2025). Symbolic infilling under hard constraints — mask a region, regenerate it, keep everything else fixed — is mature and available under permissive licences (Thickstun et al. 2024; Pasquier et al. 2025; Malandro 2024; Ens and Pasquier 2020). What remains unsolved is everything around the act of generation: how a musician states what they want, and how the machine reports what it did. The field optimised the compile step and left annotate and review almost untouched.

2. Solved enough to build on

Audio synthesis and full-song generation. Prompt to finished master, with vocals, in seconds; open weights run on consumer GPUs (Gong et al. 2026; Yuan et al. 2025; Liu et al. 2025; Evans et al. 2025).

Transcription and separation. Piano audio to MIDI is reliable (Hawthorne et al. 2018, 2019); instrument-agnostic note transcription ships as a plugin (Bittner et al. 2022); stem separation is a commodity feature.

Symbolic infrastructure. Notation rendering (Pugin, Zitellini, and Roland 2014; OpenSheetMusicDisplay contributors and Zanchi 2026), tokenisation (Fradet et al. 2021; Liao, Luo, et al. 2024), score–performance alignment (Cancino-Chacón et al. 2022) and corpus analysis (Cuthbert and Ariza 2010) are all adopt-not-build.

Constrained regeneration. The primitive the composer’s loop needs. Coconet framed it in 2017 as modelling how composers “write music in a nonlinear fashion, scribbling motifs here and there” (C.-Z. A. Huang et al. 2017); nine years on it works at bar × track granularity with ten-odd named controls (Malandro 2024; Pasquier et al. 2025), and one deployment reports musicians accepting roughly 23% of 318,000 span-level suggestions inside a notation editor (Donahue et al. 2024).

3. Working, imperfect — where the effort currently goes

Long-form structure. Deep generators still differ measurably from human music in repetition and hierarchy (Dai, Yu, and Dannenberg 2022; Dai, Zhang, and Dannenberg 2024). The remedies make form an explicit, editable input (Dai et al. 2021; Z. Wang, Min, and Xia 2024).

Controllability of audio models. Chord-, melody- and curve-conditioning exist but stay research-grade (Tal et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2023; S.-L. Wu et al. 2024; Tsai et al. 2025); no commercial full-song system accepts MIDI or chords as a generation condition. Inference-time optimisation is the most general mechanism for imposing a musical feature on a frozen model (Novack et al. 2024).

Real-time interaction. Latency crossed into usability — roughly 200 ms control latency on a laptop with open weights (Lyria Team et al. 2025) — and RL-tuned accompaniment now anticipates rather than merely reacts (Y. Wu et al. 2024, 2026; Scarlatos et al. 2025). A 2024 Aalto thesis reaches the same anticipation behaviour with a much cheaper trick: pad away part of the chord sequence so the model learns to generate melody while harmony lags (S. Wang 2024).

Expressive rendering. Score to expressive MIDI is a separable, controllable stage (Jeong et al. 2019; Y. Wu et al. 2022; Borovik and Viro 2023).

Machine music analysis. Roman-numeral analysis reaches about 51% full-label accuracy (AugmentedNet) to 62% (RNBert), with key at 82–83% (Nápoles López, Gotham, and Fujinaga 2021; Sailor 2024) — good enough to propose a label for correction, not to trust silently.

4. Contested, no consensus

Evaluation. Distributional audio metrics correlate weakly with human preference (Chung et al. 2025; Grötschla et al. 2025; Y. Huang et al. 2025), and almost no generation paper evaluates a composer’s workflow at all. The one substantial producer study reports “creative misalignment” and demands for control (Ronchini et al. 2025); co-creative systems over-measure user experience and under-measure the creativity of the collaboration (Karimi et al. 2018).

Provenance and law. Being settled in court, not in papers: memorisation held to be reproduction in Munich (Landgericht München I 2025, 2026), human authorship required for protection in the United States (U.S. Copyright Office 2025; U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit 2025), and the label settlements producing licensed, walled-garden models (Music Business Worldwide 2025a, 2025b).

Whether language models can reason about music. Every benchmark says not reliably (Li et al. 2024; Weck et al. 2024; Wei et al. 2024; Kumar et al. 2026). The emerging architecture is an LLM as orchestrator around deterministic analysers, not as the analyser.

5. Genuinely open — the thin nodes

Four taxonomy nodes are nearly empty, and they are the ones a composer inhabits.

Annotations as machine instructions (S2.4 — 4 of 463 works, none about music). All the parts exist and have never been assembled: typed, musical-time-anchored annotation records (Giraud, Groult, and Leguy 2018; Pugin, Zitellini, and Roland 2014); free-form ink and audio placed on and between staves (Cavez et al. 2025); a grammar that parses “language plus a sketch on the artifact” into typed references with per-modality resolvers, demonstrated for video (Tilekbay et al. 2024); selection-then-instruction as an interaction (Masson et al. 2024); musician-readable structural feedback to an agent (Y. Xu 2026).

The machine explaining itself (S4.4 — 2 works). A survey of 92 co-creative systems found the AI-to-human direction essentially undesigned (Rezwana and Maher 2023). The exceptions are few and instructive: a critic that says where to change before how (Ramoneda, Rocamora, and Akama 2024), committed-versus-tentative display of an agent’s plan (Scarlatos et al. 2025), explain-and-debug affordances (Louie et al. 2020).

Symbolic editing and proofreading (S4.2 — 2 works) and musical version control (S4.5 — 5 works, none with branch/merge) (Foscarin, Jacquemard, and Fournier-S’niehotta 2019; Tutteo Ltd (Flat.io) 2026; Suh et al. 2024).

Notation-native generation. No verified system emits MusicXML or MEI directly (W. Xu et al. 2025; Qu et al. 2024; Y. Wang et al. 2025), and the notation incumbents ship nothing generative (Steinberg Media Technologies 2025; Avid Technology 2026; Muse Group 2025) — DeepBach’s 2017 MuseScore plugin remains nearly the only research deployment on a notation surface (Hadjeres, Pachet, and Nielsen 2017).

Two findings make this the right place to work rather than merely an empty one. Steering interfaces move a composer’s control, ownership and self-efficacy even with the model held fixed, and interface and model gains are complementary (Louie et al. 2020; Louie, Engel, and Huang 2022); and a single slider is not control — MMM-C scored acceptable usability while its users’ desire for more control averaged 9.5 out of 10 (Tchemeube et al. 2023). Practitioners, asked directly, want to pull material rather than be pushed it, and reject automatic completion while accepting transformation of their own material (Deruty et al. 2022; Newman, Morris, and Lee 2023; Krol, Llano, and McCormack 2025; C.-Z. A. Huang et al. 2020).

6. Three projects that fit 6–12 months

A. The build log — best risk/reward. Wrap a frozen, permissively-licensed infiller (Thickstun et al. 2024) — it runs in a browser (Zhou et al. 2024) — with the missing return channel: per region, which constraints bound, which were relaxed and why, where the model was least confident, what it would offer instead. Then measure whether the explanation changes trust, ownership and suggestion acceptance rate. No model training. Instruments already exist (Cherry and Latulipe 2014). Directly addresses S4.4, the thinnest load-bearing node. CHI- or ISMIR-shaped.

B. Annotation-following: a benchmark, then a system. No benchmark exists for “did the model honour the composer’s marks?” Build the task suite, compliance metrics and baselines across existing infillers, then a compiler for one annotation type — region plus natural-language instruction on a lead sheet. Nearest precedents: rejection sampling of LLM proposals against a music prior (Kim, Lee, and Donahue 2025), zero-shot symbolic editing evaluated as unit tests (Zhang 2025). Higher ceiling than A; the benchmark alone stands as a contribution if the system slips.

C. The notation round-trip. Lossless textual IR ↔︎ MusicXML/MEI, so generation can happen in notation at all. Lower risk, lower ceiling — a resource paper, and a prerequisite for A and B. Best treated as a component.

Avoid at this timescale: symbolically-conditioned audio rendering (compute-heavy, crowded), licence-clean corpus assembly (legal legwork, though the clean sets are real (Long et al. 2025; Gotham and Jonas 2022)), version control (needs a user base before it can be studied).

The failure mode that kills all three: building a demo and running out of time before the evaluation. Fix the study design in month one.

7. Local advantage

Krzysztof Gajos is at Harvard SEAS and co-authored ChordRipple — Anna Huang’s first music paper — with her at Harvard in 2016 (C.-Z. A. Huang, Duvenaud, and Gajos 2016). His group’s work is exactly the human-AI interaction research that projects A and B require. Huang is now at MIT (MIT News Office 2026), one stop away, and Donahue’s lab at CMU is the other centre of gravity for this line (Donahue 2026; Kim et al. 2026). For the interaction-flavoured version of this work, the advising is unusually well placed.
