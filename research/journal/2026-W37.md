## The exploration

**The taxonomy's central finding.** Sorting 463 works by where they sit in a composer's working loop shows the field optimised *compile* and left *annotate* and *review* almost untouched. S3 (compile) has 125 primary entries; S2 (annotate) has 27, S4 (edit) 25. Inside those, the nodes that matter most are nearly empty: multimodal annotation as instruction (S2.4) has four entries, none about music; the machine explaining what it did (S4.4) has two; symbolic editing and proofreading (S4.2) has two. Every piece needed to fill S2.4 exists — MEI and Dezrann's time-anchored records, EuterPen's ink on staves, ExpressEdit's reference grammar for video, DirectGPT's selection-then-instruction — and none have been assembled for music. This is the opening.

**Music Transformer, and whether to reimplement it.** First position: don't — the reference implementation is TF1 `tensor2tensor`, the model is small (the paper never states a parameter count; the published config works out to roughly 20–40M), and the Anticipatory Music Transformer gives infilling around pinned material out of the box under Apache 2.0. Revised position, after pushback: for a three-month *learning* goal by someone who has built TensorFlow, reimplementing MT is correct — and it is the better research path, because owning the codebase makes grafting anticipation a week's work and makes every later modification (RoPE swap, reward-conditioned training) yours. The one genuinely hard idea is Huang's skewing trick for relative attention: O(L·D) memory instead of O(L²·D), which the paper quantifies as 8.5 GB → 4.2 MB per layer at L=2048, D=512. Plan: naive O(L²·D) implementation as the executable spec, skewed version must pass an equivalence test against it.

**The Aalto thesis.** Shenran Wang, 2024, supervised by Kaski — a real-time chord→melody improviser with three transferable pieces: pad away part of the chord sequence during training so the model anticipates harmony it hasn't heard (a cheap route to what ReaLchords gets from RL); a pitch-class-accumulation chord estimator with a 32nd-note grace window for turning live playing into a clean symbolic condition; and a direct RelSA-vs-RoPE comparison that found RoPE more musical. Entered at S5.3, verified from the full PDF, flagged as a master's thesis.

**The David Foster question.** Can AI match a sixteen-time Grammy winner? The question decomposes. Foster is pianist, composer, arranger, producer and talent-shaper, and the awards are mostly for the last three. Surface style at the piano-and-harmony layer is learnable and has been since Cope's EMI in the 1990s; casual listeners already can't tell (Deezer/Ipsos: 97%). What models cannot do sorts into *not yet* and *not in this architecture*. Not yet: form at song scale — Dai and Dannenberg's structural deficit — the runway before the key change. Not in this architecture: intent. No model represents *why*; the Foster modulation is learned as a token statistic and, trained on him, becomes a tic. Also outside scope: taste as selection (the AI Song Contest finding that humans curate), arrangement and production, artist-matching. Does AI read it? Notation, yes — solved. Meaning, no.

The reframe that came out of this: **imitation is the diagnostic, not the goal.** Fine-tune to imitate; find precisely where experts catch it; those failure modes will cluster on form and intent — which are exactly what a composer's annotations supply. What imitation can't learn from Foster tells you what the annotation layer must carry.

**From build plan to research question.** The proposed progression — piano only, melody → harmony → voicing → bebop transformation — is an excellent build plan and not yet a research project; "fine-tune on a composer, sound like them" has been done (MidiMe 2019, MIDI-RWKV 2025). The question inside it: *can a composer's style be captured and steered as separable layers rather than as one monolithic style vector, and does layered control give composers more of what they want than end-to-end transfer?* Foster and bebop become instances of a general capability. Bebopization is the sharpest piece — a *transformation* of given material (S4.2, two works), with nameable, checkable features that `rewards.py` extends to, and it is the kind of edit Krol et al. found preserves ownership.

## Decisions

- Piano first. Arrangement is a multitrack problem for later.
- Reimplement Music Transformer; graft anticipation onto it; then RoPE; then reward-conditioned training. Milestones with tests that can fail.
- Training corpus: **own Nord Stage captures** — Foster material played by hand, plus non-Foster material as a control so Foster-specific can be separated from performer-specific. Legally clean for research and the book; the compositions remain Foster's and his publishers'.
- The book framing: not *AI Learns David Foster* — *What AI Couldn't Learn From David Foster*.
- Render pipeline: FluidSynth during training for speed; the Nord's own piano engine for anything anyone will hear. MT is purely symbolic — it emits events, not audio — and that separation is a feature.
- SFT before GRPO, always. If supervised fine-tuning gets most of the way, that is a result, and it bounds how much headroom RL has.

## Gaps in our understanding

- **The reward tiering.** Strict chord-tone membership scored an F over Ebmaj7 as a miss; it's a colour tone. The reward needs chord-tone / colour-tone / avoid-note tiers weighted by metrical position, and lush extended harmony is precisely the idiom at stake. First real research problem.
- **Style-similarity evaluation is unsolved.** "Sounds like Foster" needs three layers planned before training: nameable-device detection, expert listening tests, classifiers last.
- **How much of Foster survives a piano reduction.** Unknown. The capture will tell us.
- **Whether the layered-control claim is true.** That is the thesis. We do not know yet.
- **Music Transformer's actual parameter count** — not in the paper; compute it from the config once implemented.
- **Gajos's current research focus** at Harvard SEAS — not checked against a 2026 source before naming him as a possible advisor.
