Experiment 032 · one performance, added to

One performance, a voice and strings

The owner's own solo piano recording of Theme from The Color Purple, transcribed from the audio by the ByteDance piano model; its melody and harmony then drive two additions that leave the recording untouched: a singer generated by YuE2 to the owner's lyrics "By the Road", isolated with Demucs and aligned to the piano, and strings added three ways. Every version was chosen and graded by measurement, not by ear. Thirty-second excerpts, taken where the first sung phrase begins.

The melody with words

The owner's ten-bar melody with the owner's words, "Somebody planted red bricks by the road / And its color bring the memory of the past, aa ee oo uu ee yuhuu". SoulX-Singer takes the score with one word per note (a slur where a word holds over two notes, the long G of the second phrase split so "the past" both fit); YingMusic-Singer-Plus takes the words and a sung take of the melody and places the words itself.

Research notice. The voices here are produced by open research models (SoulX-Singer, YingMusic-Singer-Plus, TechSinger) conditioned on the example voice clips that ship with those models; some of those clips appear to be excerpts of commercial recordings, so a generated voice may resemble a real artist. No artist is involved in or endorses these clips. They are thirty-second excerpts published for research commentary on singing voice synthesis, not for commercial use or redistribution.

The melody alone

Two extractions, both deterministic: the skyline of the transcription (the highest note at each attack in the right-hand register, eighths and longer) and the top note of the right-hand staff of the engraved MusicXML. The MIDI, MusicXML and scores of both, with the piano's own MusicXML and MIDI, are the files to take into a DAW.

Open-source singers that take the score

Two open models with released weights were set up for this: TechSinger (Guo et al., AAAI 2025; bilingual checkpoint with English singers), which takes phonemes, note pitches, note durations and note types, and Vevo2 (Amphion, 2026), which takes lyrics plus a melody as audio. TechSinger sings the written pitches in order; its own duration model runs at a different tempo, so a second run forces every phoneme's timing from the score, which is what "forced" means below. Vevo2 was built for a few seconds of melody and does not hold this line.

The recipe of experiment 006, on this melody

The owner judged the singers of experiment 006 good: a plain line of quarter notes, one syllable per note, lyric lines of exactly as many syllables as the notes they cover, a verse and a chorus, YuE2's own piano allowed, melody planning. The same recipe here: the one-note-per-beat melody of each sung phrase, the owner's words cut into lines that match the note counts, a baritone an octave below the piano's register and an alto at the written register, two seeds each. The first clips are YuE2's whole songs for each phrase, as generated; the last is the chosen singers aligned over the owner's piano.

Voice, the earlier route: whole songs, separated and forced

Grades are against the melody as sent to the singer: the share of its notes sung at the written pitch within 200 ms, the share at the right pitch class in any octave, how much of the pitch order survives (longest common subsequence), and precision, the share of sung notes that match a written one. A synthesised rendering of the melody itself scores 83% of notes at the written pitch and 84% of the order, which is the grader's ceiling. The retuned versions move each sung note to the written pitch with a phase vocoder: auto-tuned by construction, which is where the melody comes from in the last two.

Strings

Two string versions are written from the transcription itself (chord pads, cellos, and violins where nobody sings), timed on the beat grid tracked from the playing, so they cannot drift; the third is MusicGen Melody following the piano's chroma. The grade is the frame-wise chroma similarity between the strings and the piano, and how often the strings' strongest pitch class is among the piano's top three.
