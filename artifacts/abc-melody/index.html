A melody in ABC

ABC is a way to write music as ordinary text. A melody becomes a line of letters you can type, read, paste into a chat or feed to a program. This page explains the few rules you need, using the ten bars of Theme from The Color Purple that I wrote out myself, and shows how that clean melody tells a singing-voice model exactly what to sing.

Henry Tan · Maestro · October 2026

The melody

This is the theme the way I hear it: ten bars, no chords, no accompaniment, just the line a singer would sing.

X:1
T:Melody
M:4/4
L:1/4
Q:1/4=90
K:C
z G, C E | A, C F3/2 D/ |
E4 | Z |
z G, C E | A, C F A |
G4 | z E F G |
A2 A/ B c/ | F4 |]
The melody on its own
Sung with words by SoulX-Singer, one syllable per note

It is two answering phrases and a longer last line. The first phrase climbs G, C, E, then A, C, F and settles on a long E. After a bar of rest the second phrase starts the same way but reaches higher, to A, and settles on G. The last line climbs step by step, E, F, G, A, B, up to the high C, and drops to F. The whole melody runs from the G below middle C to the C an octave above middle C, an easy range for most voices.

How to read it

The header

The first lines start with a letter and a colon. They say how to read the notes that follow.

LineMeansHere
X:1Tune numberThe first tune in the file
T:MelodyTitleMelody
M:4/4MeterFour beats in a bar
L:1/4Default note lengthA plain letter lasts a quarter note, one beat
Q:1/4=90Tempo90 quarter notes a minute, so one beat is 0.67 seconds
K:CKeyNo sharps or flats

Pitch

Notes are letters. Capital C is middle C. A comma moves a note down an octave and a lower-case letter moves it up one.

WriteNote
G, A,G and A just below middle C
C D E F G A BMiddle C up to the B above it
cThe C above that
^F _BF sharp, B flat (not needed in this melody)

Length

A number after a note multiplies the default length. A slash divides it.

WriteLastsCalled
C1 beatquarter note
C22 beatshalf note
C44 beatswhole note
C/½ beateighth note
C3/21½ beatsdotted quarter

Rests and bars

z is a rest, and it takes lengths like a note. Z is a whole bar of rest. | is a bar line and |] ends the tune. Every bar should add up to the meter: in this melody, four beats.

Why write it this way

In my piano recording the theme sits in the middle of the texture, in the tenor register, under chords and pedal. When a program pulled the "melody" out of the recording automatically, it picked the highest notes, which were the wrong line. Ten bars of ABC fixed that in one message: no guessing which notes are the tune, no extra notes, exact lengths.

From ABC to a singing voice

A singing-voice model like SoulX-Singer does not read ABC directly. It reads a list with one row per note: the syllable, how it is pronounced, how many seconds it lasts and which pitch it is. Turning the ABC into that list is mechanical. Each beat is 0.67 seconds at 90 beats a minute, each letter becomes a MIDI pitch number, and the lyrics are split so every note gets exactly one syllable.

These are the first nine notes, with the words "Someone planted bricks by the road":

A short voice recording sets the sound of the singer. Everything else, every pitch and every length, comes from the ABC. That is why it works: the model has no room to invent its own tune. Song generators that work from a text prompt and a melody hint, such as YuE2, sang at most 27% of the notes of a longer line from the same recording. Fed this table, SoulX-Singer sang 92 to 100% of these notes at the written pitch, within a fifth of a second, in five different English voices.

Two habits make it work: write one syllable for every note, and write the words the way they are sung. Long notes are good places for open vowels ("road", "see", "ooh").

Try one

Here is the start of the Minuet in G from the Anna Magdalena Bach notebook, moved to C. It is in 3/4, so every bar adds up to three beats.

X:1
T:Minuet (opening, in C)
M:3/4
L:1/4
K:C
G C/ D/ E/ F/ | G C C | A F/ G/ A/ B/ | c C C |]

Bar 1 is one beat of G and four eighth notes. Bar 3 is the same shape a step higher, and bar 4 jumps up to the high C and back down. Paste it into any ABC editor, such as drawthedots, to see and hear it.
