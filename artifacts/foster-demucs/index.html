Experiment 030 · separate first, then listen

Foster recordings, separated first

The same twenty-four tracks as the first transcription page, read twice by the ByteDance piano transcription model (Kong et al., 2021): once from the full mix, and once from the piano stem that Demucs (Rouard et al., 2023, six-stem model) separates out first. Each row pairs the two readings of the same thirty seconds, played back by the same fixed piano soundfont.

The transcription model was trained on solo piano and has no notion of "not a piano": in the full mix, strings, synthesizer pads and voices come back as piano notes, and drums push its attack detector into false starts. Separating first removes most of that. A note counts as surviving when the stem transcription has the same pitch within 50 ms of it; a stem note is new when the mix transcription has nothing within 50 ms. Where the piano is the whole arrangement the two readings should nearly coincide; where the piano is one voice among many the stem reading should be sparser and the surviving share lower, which is the separation doing its job, not the transcription failing.

Caveats that stay: the piano stem is the weakest of the six Demucs stems, so quiet piano under strings can leak either way, and some synthesizer parts are piano-like enough to survive as piano. Key and tempo are estimated from the stem transcription on the beat grid tracked from the full mix, so both readings share one grid. Everything on this page is an excerpt (the first thirty seconds); the counts were measured on the whole tracks. The recordings and the separated stems themselves are not on this page.
