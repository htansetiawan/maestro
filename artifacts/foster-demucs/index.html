Experiment 030 · separate first, then listen

Foster recordings, separated first

The same twenty-four tracks as the first transcription page, read by the ByteDance piano transcription model (Kong et al., 2021) three times: from the full mix, from the piano stem that Demucs (Rouard et al., 2023, six-stem model) separates out first, and from the piano stem of an open band-split RoFormer trained for piano alone. Each track shows the three readings of the same thirty seconds, played back by the same fixed piano soundfont, and a ground-truth test below says which separator helps.

The transcription model was trained on solo piano and has no notion of "not a piano": in the full mix, strings, synthesizer pads and voices come back as piano notes, and drums push its attack detector into false starts. Separating first removes much of that, at the price of whatever the separator gets wrong. A mix note counts as kept when the stem reading has the same pitch within 50 ms of it; a stem note is new when the mix reading has nothing within 50 ms. On their own these shares cannot say which reading is right, which is why the ground-truth section exists.

Against ground truth the owner's solo recording under three backings

The only way to say which reading is right is a case where the right answer is known. The owner's own solo recording of the Color Purple theme transcribes cleanly (its reading is the reference, 1,010 notes). It was mixed with Demucs "everything but piano" stems of three album tracks at three levels, the backing's loudness set at −6, 0 and +6 dB relative to the piano, and each mix was transcribed straight, after Demucs, and after the RoFormer. Note F1 is against the reference (same pitch, onset within 50 ms); the SDR is how much of the true piano each separator recovered.

Caveats that stay on the album tracks: piano is the weakest stem of every public separator, so quiet piano under strings can leak either way, and some synthesizer parts are piano-like enough to survive as piano. Key and tempo are estimated from each stem transcription on the beat grid tracked from the full mix, so all readings share one grid. Everything on this page is an excerpt (the first thirty seconds); the counts were measured on the whole tracks. The recordings and the separated stems themselves are not on this page.
