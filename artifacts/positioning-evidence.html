As of September 10, 2026. Evidence labels follow the positioning-blueprint method. “Primary” means a paper, regulator, standard, or first-party product document. “Vendor-stated” means a product or scale claim made by the vendor or its commercial partner and not independently verified in-product. “Inferred” means analysis derived from the cited facts.

Key inferences to test

Inference Why it is plausible What would falsify it
Generation is becoming an embedded capability rather than a standalone differentiator Multiple scaled vendors added editing, MIDI, stems, collaboration, and DAW integration in 2025–2026 [E1–E5, E9] Buyers continue choosing primarily on raw generation quality and ignore workflow features
Score-native auditability is a better thesis wedge than another generator Frozen symbolic infillers exist [E6], score-region usage exists [E7], and objective workflow evaluation is thin [E10, E18] Interviews reveal infrequent score revision, no demand for evidence, or dominant use of audio-only workflows
Provenance can be a product asset but not a legal guarantee Human modifications matter to copyrightability [E16], and granular AI credits are being standardized [E11] Buyers will not pay, standards exclude creation-process detail, or logs fail evidentiary requirements
A separate notation editor is the wrong initial distribution strategy Existing editors already own user workflow and can add narrow AI features [E12] Plugins cannot access needed score state, incumbents block integration, or users strongly prefer a new unified editor
