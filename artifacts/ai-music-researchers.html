A working directory for Music Kitchen, checked October 17, 2026. The industry groups below identify the work these researchers contributed to, not their current employer. The final column explains why I want to follow their work.

AudioCraft and MusicGen contributors

Researcher Selected work Relevance to Music Kitchen
Alexandre Défossez Co-author of EnCodec and MusicGen. Audio compression and generation through discrete representations.
Jade Copet First author of Simple and Controllable Music Generation; EnCodec co-author. Codebook interleaving, text conditioning and melody conditioning.
Yossi Adi Co-author of EnCodec, MusicGen and AudioGen. The relationship between audio representations and controllability.
Felix Kreuk First author of AudioGen; MusicGen co-author. Text-guided audio generation and conditional modeling.

AudioCraft code · EnCodec code

Jukebox and MuseNet contributors

Researcher Selected work Relevance to Music Kitchen
Prafulla Dhariwal First author of Jukebox. Hierarchical audio compression and autoregressive song generation.
Alec Radford Jukebox co-author. Learning representations for generative audio models.
Ilya Sutskever Jukebox co-author. Historical work on large generative models for music.
Christine Payne, also known as Christine McLeavey Creator of MuseNet and Jukebox co-author; OpenAI profile. Connects symbolic music generation with later raw-audio generation.

MIT faculty

Researcher Role and work Relevance to Music Kitchen
Cheng-Zhi Anna Huang MIT Music and EECS faculty; Music Transformer, Coconet and human–AI songwriting. Long-range musical structure, editable generation and co-creation.
Eran Egozy Professor of the Practice of Music; Interactive Music Systems. Interfaces that let people participate in music without mastering notation first.
Joseph A. Paradiso Professor of Media Arts and Sciences; Responsive Environments. Musical interfaces and live interaction; the Jordan Rudess jam_bot collaboration is a relevant example.
Tod Machover Professor of Music and Media; Opera of the Future. Hyperinstruments, Hyperscore and composition interfaces that expand human expression.

Egozy, Paradiso and Machover are included for musical interaction and creative systems as well as AI-related work; their relevance extends beyond training music generators.

Other academic and research contributors

Researcher Affiliation or research context Relevance to Music Kitchen
Chris Donahue Carnegie Mellon University. Controllable music generation, Piano Genie and interactive systems for musicians and non-musicians.
Hao-Wen (Herman) Dong University of Michigan, Performing Arts Technology. Music AI and music technology; symbolic representation and generation.

Reading this week

  • Meinard Müller. Fundamentals of Music Processing: Audio, Analysis, Algorithms, Applications. Springer, 2015, first edition. Reading Chapter 1, Music Representations, pp. 1–37: sheet music, symbolic representations and audio. Publisher · Companion notebooks.
  • Jade Copet et al. Simple and Controllable Music Generation (MusicGen, NeurIPS 2023). Focus: how melody conditioning differs from exact note control. Paper.
  • Alexandre Défossez, Jade Copet, Gabriel Synnaeve and Yossi Adi. High Fidelity Neural Audio Compression (EnCodec, 2022 preprint). Focus: what the compressed audio representation retains, and why codec tokens are not readable notation. Paper.
