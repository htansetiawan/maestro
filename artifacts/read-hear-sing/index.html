Read,Hear,Sing.

楽譜から歌声へ 研究

Can a machine take a piece of music, printed on paper or recorded, turn it into notes, and sing it back? We tried this with open models and checked the results against the right answers. This page summarizes what we found.

Henry Tan · Maestro · October 2026

If you have 60 seconds

  1. ReadA 2024 reader read every note of a Mozart page degraded to look like a bad phone photo. The 2026 models did not beat it.
  2. HearOne model heard 102 minutes of David Foster records and wrote down 42,861 notes in under five minutes.
  3. SplitLifting the piano out of the band first (RoFormer) raised note accuracy from 0.55 to 0.73. Demucs made it worse.
  4. SingSoulX-Singer sang a ten-bar melody note for note (92–100%), then again in Mandarin and Korean.
  5. MissSong generators (YuE2, Vevo2) sing their own tune, not yours: 3–27%.

One question, four steps

Page · recording · melody · voice

Music arrives two ways: as ink on a page or as sound in a file. Before any model can arrange, accompany or sing a piece, it has to get the notes out. So we followed one path end to end and measured every step.

Printed page read · OMR Notes
Recording split the piano out hear · AMT Notes
A melody sing · SVS A voice

Wherever we could, we tested on pages, mixes and melodies where we already knew the right notes, so we could count how many came back correctly.

01

Reading the page

Optical music recognition

Optical music recognition turns a picture of sheet music into notes a computer can play. We lined up six readers from three generations (rule-based, 2024 neural, 2026 full-page) and made them read pages we had the answers for.

Mozart K. 545, made to look like a bad phone photo · right-hand notes read exactly

homr 2024100%
Transcoda 202696%
LEGATO 202694%
oemer U-Net74%
Audiveris rules36%

Four pages, 74 bars, rendered from an exact encoding, then blurred, skewed, unevenly lit and JPEG-crushed like a phone photo. LEGATO's number hides a habit: it reads up to 119 bars where 74 are printed.

200 real scanned lines (OLiMPiC) · notes vs. structure

homr · notes92%
Zeus · notes86%
Transcoda · notes82%

Flip to structure (rhythm, beams, layout; TEDn, lower is better) and the order flips too: Zeus 0.14, homr 0.48, Transcoda 0.56. homr gets the notes, Zeus gets the shape, and no reader does both.

The page no reader got through: Katie's Theme

From the soundtrack of Stealing Home, in the same Foster songbook. homr found 17 notes and no left hand at all. Transcoda ran away to 122 bars. LEGATO matched 12%. The page breaks the habits these readers learned from:

  • The music starts halfway down, under a large title block with Japanese text.
  • The meter changes four times in three lines (4/4, 3/4, 4/4, 2/4), and the tempo is marked "Freely".
  • Rolled chords, grace notes and slash chords like Dm7/G crowd the staves.
  • It is an old photocopy: staff lines break into dots, and the spiral binding prints as a black band down the edge.

When the page fails, go to the recording. That is where the film's love theme turns up again, two chapters on.

  • ✓

    What worked

    homr: every note on clean, photocopied and phone-photo-quality pages; 86% of the melody on a commercial Color Purple songbook.

  • ~

    Half there

    The 2026 full-page models read most notes but invent bars and sometimes loop on one measure until they run out of room.

  • ✕

    What didn't

    The readers we checked returned no chord symbols and no lyrics. Those still had to be typed by hand.

02

Hearing the recording

Automatic music transcription

Automatic music transcription does the same job by ear. I recorded myself playing the Theme from The Color Purple, and the ByteDance piano model (Kong et al., 2021) wrote it down: 1,010 notes and every pedal press, in 14 seconds. Played back, it sounds like me.

Listen · the same thirty seconds twice

Then the bigger test: 24 tracks from two David Foster albums, 102 minutes of audio, 42,861 notes found in 284 seconds on one GPU, 22 times faster than listening. Each became MIDI and MusicXML, engraved where the piano leads.

  • ✓

    Solo piano

    Close to solved. Notes, timing, loudness and pedal come back, fast.

  • ~

    Scores from audio

    Readable once we snapped notes to a beat grid and split the hands, but a free-tempo performance still needs a person to tidy it.

  • ✕

    A full band

    The model only knows piano, so strings, synths and voices come back as extra piano notes.

03

Splitting the piano from the band

Source separation

The fix seems obvious: separate the piano out first, then transcribe. But "separated" is not the same as "better", so we built a test with a known answer. I buried my own solo recording under three real backings (orchestra, a singer, a band) at three volumes, and asked each separator to give my notes back.

Note accuracy (F1) against my solo, mean of nine mixes

Clean solo1.00
RoFormer first0.73
No separation0.55
Demucs first0.48

The hardest case, a singer 6 dB louder than the piano: 0.38 with no separation, 0.35 with Demucs, 0.64 with RoFormer. RoFormer here is the band-split architecture (Lu et al., 2023) with open community weights trained for piano.

Separating first helps a lot, but it doesn't fully recover the clean piano.Best mix 0.82, against 1.00 for the solo

  • ✓

    RoFormer for piano

    Better than no separation in all nine mixes, by 0.1 to 0.3 of F1. It also passed the listening test on every record above.

  • ~

    Demucs for voices

    Its piano stem hurts, but its voice and backing split is clean and useful.

  • ✕

    Neither separator could pull out an electric piano; both were trained on the acoustic kind.

04

Singing the melody

Singing voice synthesis

Last step: give a model a melody and words, get a singer back. The melody is ten bars I wrote out myself, and the words are mine too ("By the Road"). The question is simple: does the voice sing my notes?

The melody went to the singers as ten bars of ABC, a way of writing music as plain text. How that works.

Ranges cover the voices and lyric versions we tried. YuE2 and Vevo2 were measured on the longer line from my recording, and both fell well short there. The rest sang the ten-bar melody.

  • ✓

    SoulX-Singer

    Score in, singing out: every note's pitch, length and syllable. It clones a voice from a short clip, and its voice-conversion sibling covers Korean.

  • ~

    Fitting the words

    Our first English lyrics crammed whole words onto single notes. Re-writing them one syllable per note, with "yeah" and "ooh" as filler, fixed it.

  • ✕

    Song generators

    YuE2 and Vevo2 write beautiful songs of their own, but ask them to follow a given line and they wander off it.

What we learned

Takeaways
  1. 1

    Newest isn't best.

    On our pages the 2024 readers beat the 2026 ones. Training on the right kind of pages mattered more than size or year.

  2. 2

    Every reader does half the job.

    One gets the notes, another the rhythm and layout. A reader that does both is the open gap, and the next thing to build.

  3. 3

    Clean in, clean out.

    Solo piano transcription is close to solved; a band is not. A good separator wins back about half of what the mix takes away.

  4. 4

    Control beats generation.

    Models that compose their own songs will not follow yours. Score-driven singers follow it note for note, in English, Mandarin and Korean.
